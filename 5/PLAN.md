# Plan

Phased implementation checklist for `5.p8`, based on [README.md](README.md) and [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

## Phase 1: Level generation & player movement

- [x] Row/tile data: 10 rows × 16 columns, tile types (platform/gap), generated per DESIGN.md's Level generation algorithm (gap density roll, guaranteed ladder connector per row pair, box obstacles). Rope anchor placement is deferred to Phase 3, since nothing consumes it yet — no point generating dead data early.
- [x] Render the 10 platform rows plus fixed ground row from tile data, offset by the scrolling camera
- [x] Player: left/right walk, jump (❎), gravity, collision against platform tiles, box obstacles, and the ground row, all in world-space coordinates
- [x] Ladders: climb up/down when overlapping a ladder connector column (the only vertical connector — see the Revision notes below)
- [x] Camera: `camera_y = mid(0, py-56, 224)` each frame, keeps the player roughly centred and clamped to the world's top/bottom; HUD drawn after resetting the camera offset so it stays fixed on screen
- [x] Horizontal collision: the player's left/right move is blocked by solids (in practice, only box obstacles — see the third Revision note below) instead of freely overlapping them
- [x] Automated check: a 2,000-trial Lua-harness simulation of `gen_level()` found zero cases of a missing connector, a box on a ladder's column, or a box on a gap tile
- [x] Automated check: harness tests confirmed the player is blocked at a box's side (can't walk through), can land standing on a box's top after a jump (0 of 100 timed-jump trials skipped a row this way, 10 of 100 landed on the box), and still falls through an edge-column (0 or 15) gap exactly like an interior one
- [ ] Verify: a freshly generated level is always completable ground-to-finish via ladders alone, including horizontal walkability along gap-dense rows between connector columns, which the generator doesn't explicitly guarantee (manual — needs a play test in Pico-8; see DESIGN.md's Level generation note on this known gap); also confirm the camera follow feels right (doesn't lag or overshoot) and clamps cleanly at both ends of the world; confirm jumping onto/over a box feels right now that it's actually blocked

**Revision after first play test**: the original 4-row draft (3 platforms + finish, 24px pitch, ladder-or-box connectors, a dedicated chute-lane hazard column) played too short and had boxes floating mid-air as an alternate vertical connector. First reworked to 6 rows (5 platforms + finish, 16px pitch) with ladder as the sole connector and boxes moved onto platform tiles.

**Second revision, same play-test round**: the 16px-pitch rework put the climb gap (8px) below the stated minimum of 2x the player sprite's height (16px) — cramped, not just "shorter than the original." Reworked again to a fixed 24px gap (3x sprite height, 32px pitch), which no longer fits a 10-row-equivalent level on one 128px screen; the level is now 3 screens tall (336px scrollable world) with a following camera (see DESIGN.md's World layout & camera section), giving exactly 10 rows: Platform 1 through Platform 9, plus Finish. Ladder-only connectors, on-platform boxes, and the chute-lane removal from the first revision all still hold. Jump velocity was reduced slightly in the first revision (`-3.2` → `-3`, ~12.9px apex) and remains comfortably under the new, even larger 32px row pitch.

**Third revision, next play-test round**: scrolling and the taller level both read well. Remaining issue: the player had no horizontal collision at all, so walking into a box just glitched the player onto/through it instead of requiring a jump. Fixed per DESIGN.md's new Player movement & collision section — horizontal movement now blocks against solids (box obstacles, in practice) the same way vertical movement already did. Edge-column (0/15) gap-falling was checked and confirmed already correct, no change needed there.

**Fourth revision, later play-test round**: "some spaces between platforms are too narrow to fall down." A box could land on both columns flanking a gap, and since boxes (correctly) block horizontal player movement, that made the gap unreachable on foot — jumping directly onto a single narrow gap tile past a box is unreasonably fiddly. Fixed in `gen_level()`'s box-placement step: a column adjacent to a gap tile (on either side) is now excluded from box eligibility, so every gap always has a clear walkable approach. Re-verified with a 2,000-trial harness (zero cases of a box next to a gap, on top of the pre-existing zero cases of a box on a ladder's column or a gap tile itself).

## Phase 2: Hazards & lives

- [x] Hazard spawn: timer-driven (90-frame/3s interval, level-1 value from DESIGN.md's ramp formula; per-level scaling is Phase 5), random column/type/roll-direction
- [x] Hazard fall/roll behaviour: falls through gap columns, rolls across platform columns, reversing direction only at a level edge (column 0/15); passes straight through box obstacles, rendered behind them (see the Revision note below)
- [x] Player/hazard collision: -1 life, 30-frame (1s) invincibility window after a hit; the hazard itself isn't destroyed on player contact, only on a gun hit (Phase 4)
- [x] Lives: start at 3, no cap, `dead` flag set at 0. Full End scene is Phase 5; for now `_update()` freezes and `_draw()` overlays a plain "game over" line as a placeholder
- [x] Automated check: a regression check confirmed gap fall-through still works; see the Revision note below for the box-interaction harness checks that replaced the original box-bounce ones
- [ ] Verify: hazards visibly roll along platforms, pass behind box obstacles, bounce off the level edges, and drop through gaps; a hit costs exactly 1 life even on prolonged contact; lives readout (placeholder top-left text) and the invincibility flash both look right (manual — needs a play test in Pico-8)

(The original 300-trial chute-column harness from before the Phase 1 revision no longer applies now that the chute lane is gone.)

**Revision, same play-test round as Phase 1's third revision**: hazards previously despawned on reaching the level's left/right edge and ignored box obstacles entirely. Made them bounce (reverse direction) off both, per DESIGN.md's then-updated Hazard spawn and movement section — which flagged, but didn't guard against, the risk of a hazard getting trapped bouncing forever between two boxes with no gap between them.

**Later revision, next play-test round**: "falling items get stuck between boxes, perhaps they should move behind a box unless it's at the end of a row" — exactly the flagged-but-unguarded edge case above, now hit in practice. Rather than add a generation-time guarantee against that specific box arrangement, removed the box-bounce behaviour entirely: hazards now ignore boxes and pass straight through, drawn behind them (boxes draw after hazards in `draw_playing()` now, so a box visually occludes a hazard passing under it) — matching the suggested fix, since the existing edge-bounce already handles "unless it's at the end of a row" for free (a box sitting at column 0/15 doesn't change what happens when a hazard tries to roll past the true edge). Re-verified with harnesses: a hazard between two boxes with a gap further along reaches and falls through it; a hazard between two boxes on a row with no gap at all still reaches both true edges (previously verified edge-bounce boundary values) instead of getting stuck oscillating between the boxes; player-box collision (unrelated to this fix) still works, confirmed by regression check.

## Phase 3: Rope swing

- [x] Rope anchor placement: one row-pair and column chosen per level in `gen_level()`, at the top of the climb gap, excluding that pair's ladder connector column and any column with a box on either row of the pair (see the first Revision note below)
- [x] Grab detection: player within 16px of the rope's anchor column, anywhere in the row-pair's vertical band (plus an 8px margin), pressing up — not tied to the bob's exact swing phase (see the second and third Revision notes below)
- [x] Motion: `theta = rope_amp * sin(rope_freq * time())`, continuous and automatic, not player-driven, `rope_freq=0.425` for a ~2.35s period (see second and third Revision notes)
- [x] Release (jump or fire button): `pvx`/`pvy` set from the swing's actual frame-to-frame pixel displacement at the moment of release (not a hand-tuned scale constant), then normal jump/fall physics resume — `pvx` decays as a horizontal momentum term that shares the same box-collision check as ordinary walking, so a mid-air release into a box still gets blocked correctly
- [x] Automated check: a Lua harness confirmed the rope column never collides with its pair's ladder connector, the bob's x position ranges over ~32px in a 90-frame window with nobody grabbing it, the measured swing period is ~2.35s (confirming the 15% slowdown actually took effect, not just the input constant), a 20-trial fixed-position grab test succeeded every time (worst case 1 frame), the player keeps moving frame-to-frame while attached, and after a release the player resumed falling and landed again (no stuck state, no unbounded fall)

**Revision after first play test**: rope generation excluded the ladder connector's column but not box columns, so the rope sometimes spawned on the same column as a box — overlapping it visually and, since a box blocks horizontal approach at ground height, sometimes making the rope unreachable at the position the grab check expected. Fixed per DESIGN.md's updated Level generation step 4; re-verified with a 3,000-trial harness (zero cases of a rope landing on a box column on either row of its pair, or on the ladder connector's column).

**Revision, next play test**: "the rope doesn't seem to do anything." Root cause: the original pendulum started at rest (`theta=0`, `theta_v=0`) and required the player to alternate-pump left/right to build any motion from scratch — a harness reproduction confirmed grabbing it and holding still (or not knowing to pump) left `theta` pinned at exactly 0 forever, no visible motion at all. Replaced with a continuously auto-swinging rope, per DESIGN.md's rewritten Rope swing section (the classic Pitfall! model): the bob moves on its own via `time()`, whether or not anyone's grabbing it, so there's no discoverability problem.

**Revision, next play test**: "rope now swings, but player doesn't grab it." The just-added grab check required the player to be within 14px on *both* axes of the bob's exact live position — reasonable in theory, but too fragile to hit reliably against a genuinely moving target during normal play. Replaced with a ladder-style check (column proximity + a generous vertical band, not tied to swing phase at all — see DESIGN.md's Rope swing section) and slowed the swing by 15% (`rope_freq` 0.5 → 0.425) per the same request. Re-verified: the measured period actually is ~2.35s (not just the constant), and a 20-trial harness grabbed successfully every time from a fixed approach position.

**KNOWN ISSUE, UNRESOLVED — deferred by request**: after the above revision, grabbing still doesn't work in actual play ("it didn't work"). All of this phase's harness checks (grab reliability, motion, release, landing) pass against the code as written, which means either the harness isn't reproducing what real Pico-8 input/timing does, or there's a bug the harnesses don't exercise (camera-relative coordinates during the check, an interaction with `try_grab_ladder()` running first in the same frame, `time()` behaving differently in the real Pico-8 runtime vs. the harness's `frame/30` stub, or something else not yet identified). Explicitly not fixed — picked up again later at the user's request; don't mark Phase 3 complete or move on to treating the rope as working until this is actually resolved in-game.
- [ ] Verify: a well-timed release crosses the gap and lands on the far platform; a mistimed release drops the player through the gap with no extra penalty beyond lost position; confirm grabbing now feels reliable rather than fiddly, and the slower swing still reads as a real pendulum rather than dragging (manual — needs a play test in Pico-8; **currently failing, see KNOWN ISSUE above**)

## Phase 6: Visuals & sound polish

- [x] Real sprites (not the earlier phases' flat `rectfill`) for platform, box, player (stand + walk, 2 frames), tumbleweed, barrel, hazard bottle, wagon wheel, gun, green bottle, red bottle, and finish button — authored as `tools/sprites/defs/g5_*.txt` hex-grid files, rendered to a combined preview PNG and visually reviewed before patching into the cart's `__gfx__` at indices 1-12 (see DESIGN.md's new Visuals section). - [x] SFX: all 13 events from README's Sound table (jump, ladder step, rope grab, rope release, hazard hit, green bottle, red bottle, gun pickup, gun shot, gun expiring warning, hazard destroyed, level clear, game over) encoded into `__sfx__` slots 0-12, structurally validated (correct line length/field layout, cross-checked against game 3's known-working data) since this machine can't hear audio to tune by ear
- [x] Automated check: harnesses confirmed every one of the 13 SFX slots fires at its intended trigger (jump, climb step, rope grab/release, hazard hit, green/red bottle, gun pickup/shot, hazard-destroyed, level clear, game over), that the gun-expiring warning fires exactly once (not every frame `gun_t` is near the threshold), and a regression pass confirmed the state machine, box-blocking, and level generation are unaffected
- [ ] Verify: play a full round with sound on; confirm every listed SFX fires at its correct trigger and actually sounds right, not just structurally valid (manual — needs a play test in Pico-8; sound content authored by direct encoding, not ear-judgment, since this machine can't play audio — flag anything that sounds off); confirm the sprites read clearly at actual in-game scale and the player's walk animation looks right

**Found and fixed after the first play test of this phase**: the `__sfx__` section was appended directly after `__gfx__`, skipping `__label__`. Pico-8 tolerated that on load but silently dropped the entire section on its own resave (it inserted a default `__label__` where one was missing and didn't carry forward what followed in the wrong slot) — the cart played with no sound at all despite the SFX data being correct. Re-appended `__sfx__` after `__label__` (Pico-8's canonical section order, now documented in [`../tools/CLAUDE.md`](../tools/CLAUDE.md)) and re-verified the cart parses and the SFX data is intact. Worth remembering for any future game in this repo that hand-edits `.p8` sections outside the Pico-8 editor.
